Phase 2 · accuracy study

Print the calibration object

The only honest way to say how accurate a scan is: scan something whose exact shape you know. This object is 150 × 70 mm with a dome, a ramp and two flat terraces, sized like a foot.

  1. Print it base-down at 100% scale, no supports, in a matte light colour (white or grey PLA). Download the STL.
  2. Measure it with calipers once it has cooled: overall length and width, base thickness, the dome's top height, and both terrace heights (table to top). Printers are off by a few tenths of a millimetre; your readings make the comparison fair.
  3. Scan it standing in the middle of the mat: Research capture → "Calibration object". Scan it once as printed, and once after dotting it all over with a fine marker pen (more texture), noting which in the notes.
  4. Build the 3D model from the capture library, enter your caliper readings, rebuild. The page shows the median and 95th-percentile surface error and each dimension: scan vs. calipers.

Three ways to stand

The foot isn't one shape

Clinicians disagree about which condition to capture an orthotic from. footscan captures all three and measures how much the foot changes between them, and which condition gives the most repeatable numbers.

Non-weight-bearing nwb

Setup
Lying face down, a helper holds the joint below the ankle in "neutral".
Captures
Structural shape with no load. The only view of the sole.
The argument
Classic casting reference, but "neutral" is hard to reproduce.

Semi-weight-bearing swb

Setup
Seated, foot resting on a bathroom scale. Record the reading.
Captures
Heel seated, forefoot in contact, arch not fully loaded.
The argument
Pragmatic middle ground; usually unmeasured, so here it's weighed.

Full weight-bearing fwb

Setup
Relaxed standing, weight split evenly, one foot on the scale.
Captures
What the foot actually does under load.
The argument
For a flat foot, it also captures the collapse you want to address.

The gate

No device until the noise is smaller than the correction

Scan the same foot ten times per condition. If arch height varies between scans by as much as the change you want to make, a device built from one scan is partly built from chance.

Verdict per measurement

noise = smallest real change detectable between two scans (MDC95)

PASS
noise < ½ × effect
MARGINAL
noise < effect
FAIL
noise ≥ effect: stop

Safety — requirements, not suggestions

This is not a medical device

A foot orthotic changes how load travels through the whole leg. Posting an arch wrong can move a problem to the knee, hip or back instead of solving it, and a sport like tennis compounds that quickly. These rules apply to every device this project makes and are not removed in later versions.

  1. A clinician reviews anything worn for sport. The pipeline produces a summary sheet with the scan measurements, posting angles and shell stiffness for a podiatrist, sports-medicine clinician, physical therapist or athletic trainer to sign. No sign-off, no sport.

  2. Break in slowly. One hour on day one, ramping over two weeks, walking only, with a skin check after every wear. Open the break-in tracker.

  3. Stop on any new pain. Foot, ankle, shin, knee, hip or back. Numbness, tingling, or a skin mark that hasn't faded by the next day also means stop until reviewed.

  4. No equivalence claims. A footscan device is never compared to, or described as a substitute for, a prescribed orthotic.

  5. Honest evidence. One person, unblinded: results are a case study, not proof it works.
